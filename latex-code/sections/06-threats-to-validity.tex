\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats-to-validity}

\subsection{Construct Validity (Partial)}
\label{sec:construct-validity}

\emph{Architectural information in requirements.}
User stories and acceptance criteria describe required behavior but may
omit quality attributes, deployment constraints or architectural context
needed to judge a pattern. System purpose supplies context, not sufficient
evidence of applicability by itself. The protocol retains an
insufficient-information category and requires requirement references for
positive classifications (Section~\ref{sec:applicability-experiment}).
These safeguards avoid forcing every pair into a binary decision, but
neither validate the adequacy of the supplied context nor explain the
dominant label distribution in Table~\ref{tab:applicability-results}.
Reference membership checks do not establish semantic entailment.
Independent review of the cited evidence is still needed; controlled
context enrichment is a future mitigation, not an intervention already
shown to improve classification.

\emph{Applicability, retrieval overlap and ADR quality.}
Applicability is operationalized as a model judgment under the supplied
prompt and catalog, not independently established architectural truth.
Final Top-5 overlap measures same-story candidate membership relative to
the LLM-positive set (Section~\ref{sec:rag-baseline}); it does not measure
accuracy, precision or recall. Explicit denominators and separate reporting
of classification, overlap and ADR completion limit conflation between
these constructs. ADR schema conformity and nonempty fields establish
structural completeness, not correctness, sufficient grounding or
comprehensive trade-offs (Section~\ref{sec:adr-results}).
The planned professional assessment addresses separate judgment dimensions,
but cannot convert generation completion into a validated quality measure.

\emph{Questionnaire coverage.}
The prepared Q1--Q5 items distinguish decision adequacy, grounding,
consequences, perceived record usefulness and pattern applicability
(Table~\ref{tab:expert-constructs}). This separation and the predeclared
item-specific analysis avoid treating different dimensions as an
unjustified composite score. Nevertheless, explanatory questionnaire
wording and preparation checks do not establish instrument validity or
consistent respondent interpretation. Perceived usefulness is not observed
practice improvement; the items do not directly measure implementation
feasibility, decision effort or deployment success.
Optional comments may contextualize judgments, but do not replace missing
measurements. Confirming the administered wording and case mapping,
examining actual coverage and retaining critical comments are planned
safeguards, not completed empirical validations.

\emph{Evaluation of selected pairs only.}
The prepared questionnaire presents LLM-positive pairs and their generated ADRs.
This permits assessment of those recommendations, not a catalog-wide
reference set. Rejected and insufficient-information pairs are outside
the prepared evaluation, so the design cannot establish missed applicable
patterns, false-negative rates or comparative selection accuracy.
Restricting claims to assessed cases is the current mitigation.
Independent assessment of non-positive pairs or all patterns for a bounded
subset is proposed future work (Section~\ref{sec:research-implications}),
not evidence supplied by the selected-pair questionnaire.

reconciliation pending.} After Phase 3, verify actual wording, routing,
eligibility and coverage, document deviations from the prepared protocol,
and revise these construct limits against the supported findings.
No completed instrument validation or expert-based mitigation effect is
claimed in this partial draft.

\subsection{Internal Validity (Partial)}
\label{sec:internal-validity}

\emph{Prompt design and model variability.}
The decision rules, supplied catalog fields and ADR prompt's instructional
example may influence classifications and generated decisions.
The frozen study uses one recorded model/reasoning configuration for
these stages, without a controlled prompt comparison or repeated-run
robustness analysis (Sections~\ref{sec:applicability-experiment}
and~\ref{sec:adr-generation}). No temperature or seed is supplied.
Separate requests exclude prior conversation and generated outputs,
limiting conversational carryover, but do not guarantee deterministic
responses or statistical independence across shared stories and patterns.
Prompt/source hashes and preserved outputs identify the evaluated run;
they do not isolate prompt effects or prove that another run would agree.
Controlled prompt/model comparisons and repeated executions remain
future checks, not safeguards already shown to remove these threats.

\emph{Translation and authorized corrections.}
Portuguese presentation may alter how evaluators perceive the selected
patterns and ADRs. The preparation process retains the original
requirements literally and records source text, translation candidates,
final presentation and authorized field-level corrections separately
(Section~\ref{sec:expert-preparation}). Exact mapping and preservation
checks make changes auditable, but do not independently establish
translation equivalence or exclude presentation bias.
Linguistic corrections are not architectural corrections; they must not
be interpreted as improving the generated decisions.
Actual administered text must be reconciled with these frozen versions,
with deviations reported rather than attributed to the original outputs.

\emph{Execution and retry effects.}
Strict structured outputs, identifier/reference checks, retained completed
records and provenance audits reduce malformed-output and join errors,
not unsupported reasoning. Applicability attempt reservations and its
bounded retry policy differ from ADR generation's stop-on-failure and
explicit manual retry authorization. These policies can affect which
response becomes the retained outcome; failures are not applicability
labels or extra observations. The preserved pilot boundaries and attempt
history make those choices inspectable. The authorized ADR connection-error
retry is linked to its prior attempt and yields one final record
(Section~\ref{sec:adr-results}); failed dispatch remains unknown.
Complete pair coverage limits missing-output ambiguity but does not
establish retry-neutral results, semantic correctness or an exact global
API-call count.

\emph{Evaluator learning, fatigue and analysis choices.}
Repeated cases and unequal prepared route lengths may induce learning,
anchoring or fatigue, changing judgments across a session.
System-specific routing limits the prepared workload to the selected
system, but fixed presentation order does not counterbalance order effects.
Static routing checks do not establish actual live behavior, completion
patterns or the absence of fatigue. The approved pre-inspection analysis
protocol specifies eligibility, missingness, aggregation, sensitivity
and comment coding, reducing discretion in later analysis; it is not
preregistration before collection and does not control administration.
Any amendments and prior exposure to responses must remain documented.

\subsection{External Validity (Partial)}
\label{sec:external-validity}

\emph{Industrial sample and requirement scope.}
The empirical evaluation relies on \StudySystemCount{} AI-enabled systems
(SYS01--SYS04), comprising \StudyStoryCount{} user stories
(\StudyStoriesPerSystem{} per system) and \StudyCriterionCount{} validated
acceptance criteria (Section~\ref{sec:requirement-sample}).
All four systems originate from a single public-sector organization in
Brazil. Consequently, requirements reflect specific organizational practices,
enterprise workflows, regulatory constraints, and public-administration
governance. Architectural tradeoffs prevalent in other domains---such as
high-frequency financial transactions, ultra-low-latency edge devices,
or safety-critical autonomous systems---are not represented in this corpus.
While the four systems span distinct functional capabilities (e.g., text
summarization, legal analysis, conversational assistance, and automated triage),
and the evaluation covers an exhaustive census of all \StudyAssessmentCount{}
story-pattern pairs without subsampling, the findings cannot be generalized
to all AI-enabled systems or different organizational cultures without
multi-organization replication.

\emph{Pattern catalog boundaries and coverage.}
The study uses a curated catalog of \StudyPatternCount{} architectural patterns
for AI-enabled systems drawn from literature and the SWE4AI
repository~\cite{heiland2023patterns,aiPatternsRepository}.
These patterns emphasize data management, model training, deployment, and
monitoring. The catalog does not cover general software architectural styles
(e.g., event-driven architectures, microservices, or layered designs) or
traditional quality-attribute tactics (e.g., hardware fault tolerance or
network redundancy), nor does it incorporate recent generative AI design
motifs emerging after the catalog freeze.
The structured catalog schema ensures uniform field representation
across all \StudyPatternCount{} patterns during prompting, but pattern
selection frequencies, insufficient-information rates, and generated ADR
structures may differ under alternative, expanded, or differently structured
architectural taxonomies.

\emph{Language, translation and domain context.}
The original industrial requirements and acceptance criteria were formulated
in Brazilian Portuguese, whereas the pattern catalog definitions and the
primary prompt templates are in English (Section~\ref{sec:expert-preparation}).
Model-based pattern assessment therefore requires multilingual semantic
alignment between Portuguese operational needs and English architectural
specifications. While the study preserves original Portuguese requirements
verbatim and audits translation artifacts to avoid semantic distortion,
domain-specific public-administration vocabulary and linguistic nuances
may influence prompt comprehension. The observed model behaviors and
ADR generation qualities may not transfer identically to monolingual
English requirements or other linguistic and regional contexts.

\emph{Model and pipeline specificity.}
The selection and generation pipelines rely exclusively on a single
commercial frontier model (\texttt{gpt-4o-2024-08-06}), structured JSON
generation, and a specific embedding model (\texttt{text-embedding-3-small})
for the RAG baseline (Sections~\ref{sec:applicability-experiment}
and~\ref{sec:rag-baseline}).
The resulting applicability distributions, high proportion of
insufficient-information outcomes (\StudyInsufficientInformationCount{} of
\StudyAssessmentCount{}), and specific ADR trade-off formulations reflect
the reasoning capacity, instruction-following behavior, and pre-training
distribution of this specific model snapshot.
These results do not generalize to alternative commercial models, open-weight
architectures (e.g., Llama or Mistral variants), different prompt strategies
(such as multi-step chain-of-thought or multi-agent debate), or other
retrieval configurations without direct comparative evaluation.

\subsection{Conclusion and Reproducibility Validity (Partial)}
\label{sec:conclusion-validity}

\emph{Descriptive analysis and ordinal limits.}
The quantitative findings in this study are strictly descriptive and
exploratory. Model classifications and retrieval overlaps are reported
as exact counts and percentages over clear census denominators
(Sections~\ref{sec:rq1-results} and~\ref{sec:rq2-results}).
Because the study conducts an exhaustive census of all \StudyAssessmentCount{}
story-pattern pairs across the four participating systems without random
sampling, inferential statistical tests, $p$-values, and confidence
intervals are not computed for the computational outcomes.
Similarly, the pre-declared expert evaluation protocol specifies ordinal
medians and quartile intervals rather than parametric means or composite scores,
preventing unwarranted interval-scale assumptions.

\emph{Hierarchical aggregation and small cell counts.}
The multi-level structure of the data---spanning \StudyAssessmentCount{}
assessments across \StudyStoryCount{} stories and \StudySystemCount{} systems---involves
inherent dependencies. The computational results present transparent, unpooled
story-level and system-level breakdowns alongside overall totals
(Tables~\ref{tab:applicability-results} and~\ref{tab:rag-comparison}),
avoiding Simpson's paradox or distorted conclusions driven by high-yield stories.
However, for rare outcomes (such as the \StudyRagOverlapCount{} overlapping
pairs in RQ2 or the single story SYS03-US03 with zero positive classifications),
small sample sizes limit fine-grained sub-group inferences.

\emph{Third-party model evolution and unpinned service revisions.}
The experimental pipeline depends on commercial black-box APIs hosted by
OpenAI (specifically \texttt{gpt-4o-2024-08-06} for inference and
\texttt{text-embedding-3-small} for dense retrieval).
While dated model snapshots were targeted during execution, external API
providers may apply backend infrastructure updates, unversioned floating-point
optimizations, or deprecate model snapshots over time.
Furthermore, unpinned embedding revisions could alter nearest-neighbor
retrieval rankings. Exact bit-level replication across external cloud
endpoints cannot be guaranteed independently of the service provider's lifecycle.

\emph{Artifact preservation and deterministic compilation.}
To maximize reproducibility within the boundaries of closed-model APIs, all
intermediate prompt inputs, system instructions, raw JSONL model outputs,
retrieval rankings, and final Markdown ADRs are immutably preserved with
cryptographic SHA-256 hashes and timestamped audit logs (Section~\ref{sec:ethics-reproducibility}).
All manuscript numerical values, summary tables, and protocol parameters
are programmatically compiled from verified offline data records into isolated
LaTeX macro definitions, ensuring that the reported empirical results are
fully deterministic and auditable from the preserved experimental outputs.
