\begin{abstract}
Translating requirements for AI-enabled systems into architectural choices
requires contextual pattern selection and inspectable decision rationale.
This study investigates ADR4AI, a large language model (LLM)-based approach
linking requirement-grounded pattern assessment to Architectural Decision
Record (ADR) drafts. The computational study exhaustively assesses
\StudyStoryCount{} industrial user stories and their acceptance criteria from
\StudySystemCount{} systems against \StudyPatternCount{} patterns, yielding
\StudyAssessmentCount{} story--pattern pairs. Each pair receives an applicable,
not-applicable, or insufficient-information classification; positive judgments
require supporting requirement references and supply the inputs for a Proposed
ADR. A separate frozen dense-retrieval and reranking baseline provides final
Top-\RagFinalCount{} candidates for same-story overlap comparison.
The LLM classified \StudyApplicableCount{}/\StudyAssessmentCount{} pairs
(\RqOneApplicablePercent{}\%) as applicable and
\StudyInsufficientInformationCount{}/\StudyAssessmentCount{}
(\RqOneInsufficientInformationPercent{}\%) as insufficient information.
Of the \StudyApplicableCount{} positive pairs, \StudyRagOverlapCount{}
(\RqTwoOverlapPercent{}\%) appeared in the same-story final
Top-\RagFinalCount{}. All \StudyApplicableCount{} selected pairs have exactly
one structurally complete, traceable Proposed ADR.
\textbf{TBD---Final expert participant and evidence values, and principal

These computational findings characterize model outputs, candidate overlap,
and generation completeness, not architectural correctness or comparative
superiority. Input-language and catalog-representation differences further
limit the comparison. The resulting records are inspectable material for
architect review, not a validated replacement for architectural judgment;
the expert-supported conclusion remains unresolved.
\end{abstract}
